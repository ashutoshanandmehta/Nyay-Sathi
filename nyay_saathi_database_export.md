**Use different routes for the different problems: your father can report the threats to police; ask the Circle Officer about updating the land records; and get a local property lawyer to advise on a court case if the heirs cannot agree on division.**

---

### What I'd suggest

1. A threat may amount to criminal intimidation if it threatens injury to a person, reputation or property and is intended to cause alarm or force someone to act or not act; whether these WhatsApp messages meet that test depends on their exact wording and context. BNS section 351 sets out that test. [1](#cite-1)

2. Police must record an FIR when the information discloses a cognizable offence; an FIR is not guaranteed just because a family land disagreement exists or a message is described as threatening. The police assess the information against the offence and the facts. [1](#cite-1)[2](#cite-2)

---

### What you can do today

Your father should take the messages and a written complaint to the police station covering the place where the threats happened. In the complaint, state what was said, when, who sent it, why it caused alarm, and whether anyone tried to enter the land, remove crops or force your father to do something. Ask for a receipt or complaint number and, if an FIR is recorded, a free copy.

---

### If that doesn't work

If the station refuses to record information that your father believes discloses a cognizable offence, he can send the complaint in writing by post to the Superintendent of Police for Nalanda. If that does not resolve it, the next step is an application to the local Magistrate under BNSS section 175(3); the cited procedure requires an affidavit and prior compliance with the police-station and SP steps. [3](#cite-3)[4](#cite-4)[5](#cite-5)

---

### Keep these ready

Keep the original WhatsApp chat on the phone, export or back it up if possible, and save screenshots showing the sender, date and time. Don't edit or delete the messages. Make a dated note of any related incidents and keep copies of the land papers separate from the police complaint.

---

### One thing that would help me

Do the messages threaten physical harm or damage to the land, or are they mainly pressure about who owns the land? That detail could change the police route.

### What I'd suggest

For the land, your father can take the death certificate, old land papers, revenue receipts and details identifying the plots to the Circle Officer for guidance on the mutation or land-record update. If your uncle contests who inherited what or refuses division, have a property lawyer review the title and advise on the appropriate court process rather than treating a revenue-record change as a final resolution of the family dispute.

Because the land is still recorded in your grandfather's name and your uncle disputes division, a local property lawyer can check the title papers, identify all heirs and confirm the right court route. Your father should take the original papers and copies, the death certificate, revenue receipts, plot details and a list of close surviving relatives.
